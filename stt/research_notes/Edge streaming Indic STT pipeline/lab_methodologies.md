## Q1. Per-lab: core recipe, data scale, compute, Hindi/Telugu/code-switch results, and transferable lesson at small budget

### Takeaway
Nobody at the frontier trains cheaply: the headline recipes use hundreds of thousands to tens of millions of (mostly pseudo-labeled) hours and tens-to-hundreds of A100/H100. The transferable pieces are (a) NVIDIA's cache-aware streaming FastConformer + hybrid RNNT/CTC + language-ID prompt conditioning, which is already released as a 0.6B streaming checkpoint that includes Hindi (Telugu not confirmed), (b) AI4Bharat's open Indic data/models as teacher, init and data source, and (c) Granary-style pseudo-labeling hygiene (hallucination filtering) applied with an Indic-capable teacher. Delayed-Streams / audio-LLM approaches are accurate but too large/GPU-bound and lack Telugu.

### Cited Findings

#### Kyutai (Delayed Streams Modeling, Mimi, DSM-ASR)
- DSM models time-aligned streams with a decoder-only LM; alignment is done in preprocessing and delays are inserted between streams; for ASR the text stream is delayed. Paper v1 10 Sep 2025, v2 29 Sep 2025; arXiv listing is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (that is the paper's license). — [arXiv 2509.08753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08753)
- Mimi: 24 kHz audio, 12.5 Hz frame rate, RVQ with up to 32 codebooks of 2048 entries. — [DSM paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2)
- DSM-ASR pretraining: about 2.5M hours of public English+French audio, pseudo-labeled with whisper-timestamped; fine-tuning on about 28k hours of ground-truth public sets (LibriSpeech, VoxPopuli, GigaSpeech, AMI, SPGISpeech, TED-LIUM, Earnings22); long-form: ~1k h concatenated LibriSpeech + ~22k h of synthetic dialogs from a preliminary DSM-TTS. — [DSM paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2)
- Compute (main 2.6B model): pretraining 1.6M steps on 48 H100; fine-tuning 100k steps on 16 H100; long-form adaptation 25k steps on 16 H100. A 300M variant exists (Appendix B). — [DSM paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2)
- Fixed ASR delay 2.5 s for the 2.6B English model; variable-delay model samples 0.25-4 s. Average short-form WER 6.4% vs 7.5% Whisper-large-v3 offline and 9.4% streaming Whisper; long-form 7.9% vs 7.8% Parakeet-0.6B-tdt-v2. — [DSM paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2)
- The paper does not explain why en/fr only and does not discuss adapting DSM to other languages; its stated limitation is that DSM needs time-aligned data, which reduces the supply of gold training data. — [DSM paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2)
- Released models: stt-2.6b-en (English only, 2.5 s delay, weights CC-BY 4.0, pretrain on 2.5M h with whisper-timestamped labels, fine-tune ~24k h [paper says ~28k h: minor conflict], Mimi 12.5 Hz x 32 audio tokens/frame); stt-1b-en_fr (~1B, 0.5 s delay, semantic VAD, fine-tuned on 2,000 h Fisher + proprietary 1,000 h EN / 600 h FR). — [kyutai/stt-2.6b-en](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-2.6b-en)
- No official Kyutai STT fine-tuning code or new-language announcement was found; the official repo describes inference. A community Icelandic streaming model was reportedly fine-tuned from stt-1b-en_fr by extending the text vocabulary (secondary-source; Moshi-finetune repo notes vocab-size changes are not supported). — [search summary: Kyutai repo/Hugging Face](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/delayed-streams-modeling); [kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr) [UNVERIFIED Icelandic details]
- Model card for 2.6B shows a CUDA example, no CPU/MLX path mentioned there. — [kyutai/stt-2.6b-en](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-2.6b-en)
- Voxtral Realtime (Mistral, arXiv 2602.11298, Feb 2026) builds on DSM: 4B-class natively streaming model, 13 languages, Apache 2.0; encoder 970M causal (trained from scratch), decoder initialized from Ministral 3B; delay sampled 80-2400 ms; Hindi FLEURS WER 14.26% (240 ms), 12.88% (480 ms), 11.82% (960 ms), 10.73% (2400 ms); Telugu not in the language list; total ~4.4B params; evaluated on vLLM, no edge testing. Training-data hours not disclosed ("large-scale dataset spanning 13 languages"). — [Voxtral Realtime HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11298v2); [HF model card via search](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)

#### NVIDIA NeMo (FastConformer, TDT, Canary/Parakeet, cache-aware streaming, Granary, Nemotron streaming)
- Stateful (cache-aware) Conformer: streaming models are trained from scratch with limited context (still in non-autoregressive mode); three masking schemes: zero look-ahead, regular look-ahead, chunk-aware look-ahead (best accuracy/latency). LibriSpeech test-other RNNT: offline 5.0%, buffered streaming 11.3% (2000 ms), cache-aware chunk-aware 6.3% (1360 ms), zero look-ahead 9.5% (0 ms). — [Stateful Conformer, arXiv 2312.17279](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17279v3)
- One model can serve multiple latencies by sampling chunk size per batch; matched or beat single-latency models at 40/240/520 ms. Hybrid CTC/RNNT shares an encoder with loss = 0.3*CTC + RNNT. Models ~114M params, >=200 epochs, effective batch 2048-4096, data: LibriSpeech + NeMo ASRSET 3.0 (~26k h). Paper does not say whether offline checkpoints can be fine-tuned into streaming. — [Stateful Conformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17279v3)
- Granary (arXiv 2505.13404): open pseudo-labeling pipeline, 25 European languages; steps: segmentation (Whisper timestamps for YODAS/YTC, Parakeet timestamps for LibriLight; 40 s max), two-pass Whisper-large-v3 (pass 1 language ID, pass 2 transcribe) with Silero VAD, hallucination filters (repeated n-grams, long words, frequent hallucinated phrases), character-rate and character-set filters, LID filtering, punctuation/capitalization restoration with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (rejected if >5% CER change). Retention 47.9-74.6% depending on corpus. Result: ~50% less data for similar quality; a FastConformer-L (~120M) hybrid RNNT-CTC was the ablation model. Dataset license not confirmed from the paper page. — [Granary](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13404)
- Canary-1B-v2 / Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 (arXiv 2509.14128): 25 European languages (no Hindi/Telugu); ~1.7M h total (Granary ~996k h + NeMo ASR Set 3.0 ~227k + supplementary ~478k + non-speech ~37k). Single unified SentencePiece BPE (vocab 8,192 or 16,384 tested; unified beat concatenated tokenizers, no numbers given). Canary stage 1 on 64 A100 for 150k steps (LR 4e-4); stage 2 100k steps; fine-tune 10k steps LR 2e-5. Parakeet-v3: 150k steps on 128 A100 then 5k steps on 4 A100 using 7,500 h. Dynamic two-level data balancing (alpha=0.5 corpora, beta=0.5 languages) with annealing toward uniform in fine-tuning; FLEURS ASR WER 11.03% -> 8.40% with the balancing schedule; no streaming inference described (long-form via 30-40 s parallel chunks). — [Canary-1B-v2 / Parakeet-v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2)
- Nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b (released 5 Jan 2026, updated 13 Mar 2026): cache-aware FastConformer 24-layer + RNN-T, 600M params; ~250k h ASRSet (530k h total incl. YouTube-Commons, YODAS2, LibriLight, Mosel, etc.), hybrid human/pseudo labels; chunk configs att_context_size [70,0/1/6/13] = 0.08/0.16/0.56/1.12 s with avg OpenASR WER 8.43/7.67/7.07/6.93; NVIDIA Open Model License, commercial OK; initialization and streaming-training procedure not stated; local inference via quantized GGUF (q8_0) + NeMo-Speech.cpp, no CPU performance claim. — [nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)
- Nemotron-3.5-ASR-Streaming-0.6B (model card dated 4 Jun 2026): extends the English model with language-ID prompt conditioning (one-hot language vector broadcast over time, concatenated with encoder output, projected to the decoder); 40 language-locales; Hindi (hi-IN) supported, Telugu not listed; FLEURS Hindi WER with LID 8.13% (80 ms) to 6.81% (1.12 s), 11.47%-8.23% with auto-detect; Hindi WER 20.2 on Vaani-Benchmark-V1.0; data blend includes proprietary Riva multilingual set, Granary, MLS, Common Voice, FLEURS, VoxPopuli/Europarl; some synthetic labels from an ensemble of ASR models; punctuation from Qwen3-32B; 10k-1M hour bracket; license stated as OpenMDW-1.1 and "ready for commercial use". — [nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b)
- Conflict on Nemotron 3.5 license: the HF card says OpenMDW-1.1; Together AI listing says NVIDIA Open Model License. — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b); [Together AI listing](https://www.together.ai/models/nemotron-3-asr-streaming-0-6b)
- NVIDIA FAQ (vendor claim): 10-50 h of domain audio gives measurable WER gain, 20-100 h for accent adaptation, "a few hundred hours" supports adding a new language via transfer learning. The model card says 8 locales are recognized by the tokenizer but need fine-tuning to unlock (whether Telugu is among them is not verified). — [NVIDIA Nemotron-speech FAQ](https://perspectives.nvidia.com/nemotron-speech/task/faq/how-much-training-data-do-i-need-to-fine-tune-a-multilingual-streaming-asr-model/); [HF card via search summary](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b)
- Community data point: a Danish fine-tune from the Nemotron 3.5 base went from ~35.8% to ~16.0% WER (Common Voice, small speaker-disjoint test set) with ~10 h of data. Single anecdote. — [LokaalHub/nemotron-3.5-da](https://huggingface.co/LokaalHub/nemotron-3.5-da)
- NVIDIA April 2026 paper: unified offline+streaming RNNT with chunk-limited attention + right context, dynamic chunked convolutions, and a mode-consistency regularization (MCR-RNNT, Triton kernel) that narrows the offline-streaming gap while preserving offline accuracy; English checkpoint and framework open-sourced. Numbers not read. — [arXiv 2604.19079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19079)
- NeMo docs: fine-tuning can load the encoder+preprocessor and exclude the decoder (needed when changing tokenizer); a NeMo tutorial exists for fine-tuning a transducer on Telugu FLEURS. — [NeMo fine-tuning docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/speech/latest/asr/fine_tuning.html) (via search snippet; the specific tokenizer-change section was not read)

#### AI4Bharat (IndicConformer, IndicWav2Vec, IndicWhisper, Vistaar, IndicVoices, Shrutilipi)
- IndicVoices: 7,348 h (read 9%, extempore 74%, conversational 17%) from 16,237 speakers, 145 districts, 22 languages, of which 1,639 h transcribed at paper time (Findings of ACL 2024); a later dataset listing gives 12,000 h / 22,563 speakers / 208 districts (version conflict). The paper includes monolingual and code-switched speech and an open data-collection blueprint; used to build IndicASR. — [search summary of IndicVoices paper](https://aclanthology.org/people/tahir-javed/); [AI4Bharat datasets listing](https://admin.models.ai4bharat.org/datasets/)
- IndicConformer: per-language models ~120M params (Conformer-Large, 17 blocks, d=512, hybrid CTC+RNNT, MIT); 600M multilingual model covers 22 languages, user selects language code and CTC or RNNT decoding; model card gives no training-data description, no streaming mention; Hindi 13.2 WER on Vaani-Benchmark-V1.0, no Telugu number on the card; gated download. — [indic-conformer-600m-multilingual](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual); [IndicConformer repo summary](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR)
- Independent comparison (SraVaani paper, 2026): mean WER Hindi 13.8 (8 datasets, 51.6 h) and Telugu 26.4 (6 datasets, 17.8 h) for IndicConformer; Gemini 3 Flash 14.5 / 28.9, Sarvam Saaras v3 14.5 / 28.7. — [SraVaani 1.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235)
- Vistaar / IndicWhisper (Interspeech 2023): Vistaar-Train 10,736 h over 12 languages (Hindi 2,150 h, mostly Shrutilipi 1,620 h; Telugu 806 h: Shrutilipi 390, NPTEL 183, Kathbath 142). One Whisper-medium (769M) fine-tuned per language, original byte-level BPE tokenizer kept; hyperparameters/GPUs not reported (Param Siddhi supercomputer). Average WER Hindi 13.6 (vs Nvidia-large 18.6, Azure 20.0, IndicWav2Vec 21.0, Google 23.9) and Telugu 28.8 (vs IndicWav2Vec 30.2, Azure 31.4, Google 42.4). IndicWhisper beats IndicWav2Vec (317M wav2vec, trained on Kathbath) on 45 of 59 benchmarks. No ablations on multilingual vs monolingual. — [Vistaar](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15386)
- Telugu per-benchmark IndicWhisper WER (README): Kathbath 25.0, Kathbath-hard 27.8, FLEURS 25.4, IndicTTS 33.8, MUCS 32.1. — [Vistaar GitHub README via search](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/vistaar)
- Robustness study across Indic models: competing background speech is the worst degradation; Whisper-based models are more robust than Conformer-based under babble; GSM (2G) codec worst; neural audio restoration increased WER. — [Factors affecting ASR performance, arXiv 2606.09335](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09335)

#### ARTPARK-IISc (Vaani) / SraVaani
- SraVaani-1.0 (arXiv 2608.08235, Aug 2026; MIT per HF card): FastConformer, 17 layers, d=1024; hybrid TDT+CTC (CTC weight 0.3), shared 5,000-unit SentencePiece BPE across all scripts; (1) wav2vec2-style SSL pretraining on 28.4k h of unlabeled Vaani, (2) audio-image alignment on 11.8M pairs (16.6k h; frozen SigLIP2), (3) supervised fine-tuning on 30.6k h from 24 public sources, 65 languages (Hindi 3,615 h, Telugu 2,036 h), LR 1e-5, bf16. Mean WER Hindi 14.0, Telugu 25.1 (best on Telugu in its table). No ablations, no code-switch results (code-switched transcripts were removed from fine-tuning data), no streaming, no compute reported. — [SraVaani 1.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/ARTPARK-IISc/SraVaani-1.0)
- Audio-image alignment as a continued-pretraining stage: FLEURS 11 Indian languages, overall WER 66.42% -> 49.77% (25% relative), compute-matched control 65.99%, shuffled pairs 72.60%; gains largest at 10-100 h fine-tuning budgets. Base encoders not named on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2606.24080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24080)

#### Meta (Omnilingual ASR, MMS, wav2vec2/XLS-R)
- Omnilingual ASR (arXiv 2511.09690, Nov 2025): SSL wav2vec2 encoder scaled to 7B (300M/1B/3B/7B variants); ~4.3M h SSL audio (3.84M h with LID across 1,239 languages + 460k h without); two decoders: CTC (single linear layer) or LLM-style transformer decoder; language-and-script code embedding with random dropout so it works with or without it; labeled data table lists open sets 15,000 h, internal/licensed 150,100 h, etc. (table totals are internally inconsistent: rows sum ~177.7k h vs stated 120.7k h); in-context-learning zero-shot extension to new languages (stated to be below fully trained systems); compute not reported; no Hindi/Telugu numbers were extracted. — [Omnilingual ASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09690) (only first ~100k chars of 253k read)
- Release (Dec 2025 update): Apache 2.0 code and models; CTC 325M-6.5B, LLM 1.6B-7.8B; CTC-300M ~2 GiB VRAM, RTF 0.001 on A100 (batch 1, 30 s); LLM 300M ~5 GiB; fine-tuning recipes provided for CTC and LLM (not for Unlimited); inference limited to <40 s audio; no streaming mentioned; no CPU notes. — [omnilingual-asr GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr)
- MMS and XLS-R were not separately fetched. [GAP]

#### OpenAI Whisper and distillation
- Zero-shot base Whisper is weak on Indic: an Adalat AI benchmark post reports zero-shot WER sometimes >100% for Hindi and routinely >100% for Malayalam (script mismatch, scant Indic pretraining). — [Adalat AI Vividh-ASR post](https://huggingface.co/blog/adalat-ai/vividh-benchmark) (via search summary)
- Sony Research India (ICASSP 2025): Whisper's tokenizer yields many tokens per Indic word (slow inference); prompt-tuning with language-family info plus a reduced-token tokenizer improves accuracy/speed across Small/Medium/Large. — [Sony paper page](https://ai.sony/publications/Enhancing-Whisper's-Accuracy-and-Speed-for-Indian-Languages-through-Prompt-Tuning-and-Tokenization)
- Whisper-large-v3 shows Telugu "script collapse" (output in wrong script) that a per-language LoRA corrected; for Hindi and Tamil vanilla Whisper already scores >=0.98 on the script-fidelity metric. — [arXiv 2605.03073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03073)
- Whisper-medium fine-tuned on Kathbath Telugu (141.8 h): 23.31% WER; with IndicNLP morphological splitting of targets 18.73%; best with decoder modifications + splitting 16.69% (2026 paper). Hindi ~10.7% in the same paper's snippet. — [arXiv 2606.09535](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09535)
- Vendor claim (Deepgram, Nov 2023): large-v3 hallucinates more than v2 on real-world audio (median WER 53.4 vs 12.7), examples Japanese/Korean, not Indic; OpenAI card itself flags hallucination risk. Treat vendor numbers cautiously. — [Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/learn/whisper-v3-results)
- Multilingual DistilWhisper (Naver): distils whisper-large-v2 into whisper-small plus language-specific experts; authors claim it beats standard fine-tuning and LoRA in targeted languages; whether Hindi/Telugu were targeted was not confirmed. No Indic evaluation of distil-whisper was found. — [arXiv 2311.01070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01070)

#### Audio-LLM ASR (Qwen3-ASR, Voxtral, Omnilingual-LLM, Saaras V4)
- Qwen3-ASR (arXiv 2601.21337, Jan 2026; Apache 2.0): AuT attention-encoder-decoder encoder (300M with 1.7B model, 180M with 0.6B), 12.5 Hz tokens, dynamic flash-attention window 1-8 s; AuT pretrained on ~40M h pseudo-labeled (mostly zh/en); Omni pretraining on 3T tokens; SFT; GSPO RL on ~50k utterances. 30 languages + 22 Chinese dialects; Hindi yes, Telugu no. FLEURS Hindi WER 17.15 (1.7B) / 19.12 (0.6B). Streaming: dynamic window, 2 s chunks, 5-token fallback, last 4 chunks unfixed; streaming averages 3.33 vs 2.69 offline for 1.7B on LibriSpeech/FLEURS-en/zh only (no Hindi streaming numbers). — [Qwen3-ASR report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337); [search summary](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B)
- A third-party Tamil fine-tune reports base Qwen3-ASR-1.7B scoring 153% WER on FLEURS-Tamil (unsupported language), suggesting unsupported Indic languages need real training, not just adaptation. — [osmapi/tamil-asr-qwen3](https://huggingface.co/osmapi/tamil-asr-qwen3)
- A newer Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR report (arXiv 2609.07549) describes a dedicated low-latency streaming model, 30 languages; Hindi/Telugu coverage not checked. [UNVERIFIED] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07549)
- Sarvam Saaras V4 (blog, 2026): audio encoder + downsampling adapter + 3B hybrid state-space LLM decoder "completely trained from scratch in-house"; output modes verbatim / transcribe / codemix (native script with English words in Latin script) / translit / translate; claims <150 ms time-to-first-token WebSocket streaming; no data sizes or per-language WER given; weights not open. — [Saaras v4 blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/introducing-saaras-v4)

#### k2 / Next-gen Kaldi, Moonshine, Google USM
- Zipformer (ICLR 2024): U-Net-like encoder at multiple frame rates, BiasNorm, Swoosh activations, ScaledAdam; "faster, more memory-efficient, better-performing" than Conformer (abstract, no numbers on page); code in k2-fsa/icefall. — [arXiv 2310.11230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11230)
- A community streaming Hindi/Hinglish Zipformer-M CTC (64.6M params, Apache-2.0, icefall, single NVIDIA L4, bf16): from scratch on ~89 h gave 37.2% WER on IndicVoices-hi; warm-start on ~337 h IndicVoices-hi (+3x speed perturbation) gave 25.7% IndicVoices-hi, 39.9% Hinglish call-test, 44.5% Svarah (Indian English); causal chunked encoder. This is a hobbyist model card, not a peer-reviewed result. — [Vengadanathan/hindi-hinglish-zipformer-ctc-streaming](https://huggingface.co/Vengadanathan/hindi-hinglish-zipformer-ctc-streaming)
- Moonshine v2 (arXiv 2602.12241, Feb 2026): sliding-window (ergodic) streaming encoder; sizes 34M/123M/245M; English only (multilingual variants "future work"); ~300k h training (200k h original Moonshine setup + 100k h internal), 400k steps, batch 512, 8 H100; encoder windows (16,4) outer and (16,0) middle layers = 80 ms lookahead; Apple M3 response latency 50 ms (Tiny) / 148 ms (Small) / 258 ms (Medium) vs 11,286 ms for Whisper-large-v3; open-ASR avg WER 12.01 / 7.84 / 6.65; license described only as "permissive". Abstract versions conflict (6x vs 4x model-size claim). — [Moonshine v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12241)
- Google USM: 12M h unlabeled audio, 300+ languages, SSL with random-projection quantization + speech-text modality matching; labeled data ~1/7 of Whisper's yet comparable/better; >100 languages. Context only; closed. — [arXiv 2303.01037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01037)

#### Academic code-switching methods
- MUCS 2021 (Interspeech 2021): 7 Indian languages incl. Telugu (monolingual), plus Hindi-English and Bengali-English code-switching; baselines 30.73% / 32.45% WER. Reported top systems: CSTR (multilingual model on Bengali+Hindi lectures, LM from CommonCrawl + in-domain English, transliteration for English pronunciations); JHU-GoVivace (pronunciation modeling, data cleaning, web-crawled LM; transliterated WER 19.5% / 23.2% dev); Hussein et al. (monolingual pretraining then fine-tune on noisy CS data, 3rd in CS track; leading two systems reportedly crawled YouTube and learned transliteration pairs). Which team won was not confirmed. — [MUCS overview](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/diwan21_interspeech.html); [CSTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05885v2); [Kumar et al. dual-script](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/kumar21e_interspeech.html); [Hussein et al.](https://awesomepapers.io/speech-audio/papers/hussein2021balanced)
- Chadha et al. 2022: LID + monolingual models, WER 21.77 (Hi-En) and 28.27 (Bn-En). Verma et al. Interspeech 2023: fine-tune pretrained models on Devanagari-transliterated text for noisy code-mixed calls; deployed in call centers. — [Chadha et al., arXiv 2203.16578](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16578); [Verma et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/verma23_interspeech.pdf)
- Interspeech 2025 (Biswas et al.): Whisper adaptation for low-resource Hindi-English code-mixed speech via language prompts in fine-tuning, on-the-fly code-mixed data simulation, and Llama-generated code-switched text paired with synthesized audio. — [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/biswas25_interspeech.html) (abstract-level only; numbers not read)
- Language-aware adapters / refined encoder for Whisper code-switching (Mandarin-English SEAME): 4.1% and 7.2% relative MER reduction on dev sets. Soft prompt tuning for code-switching matches LoRA-level parameter efficiency with further error reductions. "Adapting the adapters" (Kulkarni et al. 2023): fusing language adapters with a latent binary frame-level gate gave >=10% absolute CER reduction on Arabic/Mandarin/Hindi-English. — [arXiv 2412.16507](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16507); [arXiv 2506.21576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21576v1); [Kulkarni et al. via search](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/biswas25_interspeech.html) [UNVERIFIED paper link for Kulkarni]
- A 2025 preprint "CLEAR" proposes LLM rescoring for code-mixed Indian ASR (S-WER 26.9). Only abstract-level info seen. — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16578) [UNVERIFIED]
- Open Telugu-English code-switched speech: none found that is clearly openly licensed. IndicVoices contains code-switched speech and was used by the CoSTA paper to pull Telugu-English data; license not confirmed. A 200 h Telugu-English corpus exists but access is by request. MUCS has no Telugu-English. — [CoSTA, arXiv 2406.10993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10993); [MUCS data paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00235v1)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The only labs whose released artifacts match "streaming + CPU-class + commercial + fine-tunable" are NVIDIA (cache-aware FastConformer 0.6B; Hindi in 3.5) and k2 (Zipformer, small). DSM/Voxtral (GPU-bound, 1-4B), Qwen3-ASR (no Telugu), Omnilingual (no streaming, <40 s) and Whisper are better used as teachers or components than as the deployed model.
- [INFERENCE] Data scale dominates: every Indic result above that approaches ~13-14% Hindi / ~25% Telugu WER used thousands of hours (Hindi 2-3.6k h, Telugu 0.8-2k h). The ~337 h Zipformer from-scratch result (25.7% Hindi on IndicVoices, 39.9% Hinglish) shows what a one-GPU, few-hundred-hour from-scratch budget buys; starting from a strong multilingual encoder is essential.
- [INFERENCE] Code-switching is the least-solved piece in published work: SraVaani removed code-switched transcripts, IndicConformer has no CS evaluation, Saaras V4 reports no CS WER. A proprietary-quality CS system will need synthetic or self-built CS data plus an output-script policy (native vs Latin for English words) chosen up front.

- No Hindi/Telugu numbers for streaming Qwen3-ASR; Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR language coverage unchecked.
- No technical reports with ablations for Sarvam, Gnani, Krutrim, Soket, Bhashini.

## Q2. Best accuracy-per-dollar approach for adding/strengthening Telugu + Hindi + English code-switch in an existing model

### Takeaway
The best-supported recipe is: start from an already-streaming multilingual FastConformer (Nemotron-3.5-streaming, Hindi present) or an Indic-strong offline model, fine-tune (full or high-rank LoRA on the encoder, new decoder/tokenizer for Indic scripts) on a few hundred to a few thousand hours of open Indic data balanced with English, and add synthetic/pseudo-labeled code-switch data. Evidence on each lever is thin and mostly indirect; the strongest quantitative lever found is domain-matched synthetic data (for entity-dense code-mixed speech), then morphology-aware tokenization (Telugu), then SSL/audio-image continued pretraining only if unlabeled in-language audio is available.

### Cited Findings
- Fine-tuning, not zero-shot: base Whisper and Qwen3-ASR on unsupported/under-covered Indic languages is catastrophic (Whisper >100% WER on some Indic, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B 153% on Tamil). — [Adalat AI](https://huggingface.co/blog/adalat-ai/vividh-benchmark); [Tamil Qwen3 fine-tune](https://huggingface.co/osmapi/tamil-asr-qwen3)
- Data amounts that worked in published Indic fine-tunes: Whisper-medium on 141.8 h Kathbath Telugu -> 23.3% WER (Kathbath test); IndicWhisper with 806 h Telugu -> 28.8% mean across benchmarks (harder, mixed test sets, not directly comparable); SraVaani Telugu 2,036 h -> 25.1% mean across 6 sets. — [arXiv 2606.09535](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09535); [Vistaar](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15386); [SraVaani](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235)
- Tokenizer: Canary-v2 found a unified BPE beat concatenated tokenizers and larger vocab was better (4,096 < 8,192 ~ 16,384); SraVaani uses one 5,000-unit BPE shared across all Indic scripts; Whisper's byte-level tokenizer is inefficient on Indic. Morphological splitting of Telugu targets (IndicNLP) cut Whisper-medium WER 23.31 -> 18.73 (plain FT) and 21.61 -> 16.70 (best decoder variant) — note splitting changes the output form so it must be reversible/consistent at evaluation. — [Canary-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2); [SraVaani](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235); [Sony](https://ai.sony/publications/Enhancing-Whisper's-Accuracy-and-Speed-for-Indian-Languages-through-Prompt-Tuning-and-Tokenization); [arXiv 2606.09535](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09535)
- Language conditioning: Omnilingual (language+script code with random dropout), Nemotron 3.5 (one-hot LID broadcast to decoder; with-LID Hindi FLEURS 8.13%/6.81% vs auto-detect 11.47%/8.23%), Whisper language prompts in Hindi-English CS (Biswas 2025). Supplying the language/script reduces error; auto-detect costs ~3 points absolute on Hindi at 80 ms. — [Omnilingual](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09690); [Nemotron 3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b); [Biswas et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/biswas25_interspeech.html)
- Synthetic code-mixed data: ~22,000 entity-dense Indic-English utterances synthesized with an open Indic TTS pipeline for under $50; a Telugu LoRA on vasista22/whisper-telugu-large-v2 raised entity-hit-rate from 0.027 to 0.473 (commercial Deepgram Nova-3 0.16) on a synthetic held-out set, native-speaker check n=20 gave 0.516; a LoRA on FLEURS-Te alone reached only 0.020; but read-speech FLEURS-Te WER regressed by up to +6.6 points; targets missed (pre-registered 0.75 Telugu, Hindi 0.337 below Deepgram). Single preprint; entity-hit-rate, not general WER. — [arXiv 2605.03073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03073)
- LoRA vs full FT: mixed evidence, none Indic and none with measured forgetting on the original languages. Frisian: LoRA (~1.4% params) comparable to full FT; Swiss German: full FT 25.60% vs LoRA 26.28% (best runs) and the authors say both comparable when LoRA is configured properly; a 2026 low-resource study: LoRA rank 128 approaches full FT, rank 8 is 2-8% (WER, unit as reported) worse; Nepali: PEFT cut trainable parameters ~99.7% but WER was clearly worse than full FT on one report. — [Frisian thesis](https://campus-fryslan.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/539); [Swiss German, arXiv 2606.07608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07608); [low-resource FFT vs LoRA, arXiv 2609.29800](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29800); [Nepali LTEDI 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ltedi-1.31.pdf) (all via search summaries)
- LoRA in sequential/new-language settings still forgets without extra measures (O-LoRA, EWC studied for Whisper). — [arXiv 2408.10680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10680) (via search summary)
- Pseudo-labeling at scale works but needs filtering: Kyutai (2.5M h with whisper-timestamped), Qwen3-ASR (40M h pseudo-labeled), Granary (Whisper-large-v3 two-pass + hallucination, char-rate, LID, charset filters; 47.9-74.6% retention; ~50% less data for equal quality), Nemotron 3.5 (ensemble-ASR labels + Qwen3-32B punctuation). — [DSM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2); [Qwen3-ASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337); [Granary](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13404); [Nemotron 3.5](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b)
- Whisper-large-v3 is a poor Granary-style teacher for Telugu (script collapse). — [arXiv 2605.03073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03073)
- Continued pretraining without transcripts: audio-image alignment gave 25% relative WER reduction on FLEURS in 11 Indic languages with compute-matched and shuffled controls showing the effect is not just extra compute; strongest at 10-100 h labeled budgets. Requires paired audio-image data (Vaani). — [arXiv 2606.24080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24080)
- Dynamic data balancing (language/corpus temperature sampling, annealed to uniform in the final fine-tune) improved FLEURS ASR WER 11.03% -> 8.40% and CoVoST2 ASR 10.33% -> 8.85%. — [Canary-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2)
- Code-switching techniques with reported gains: language-aware adapters + refined encoder (4.1%/7.2% relative MER, Mandarin-English); fused language adapters with frame-level gate (>=10% absolute CER, incl. Hindi-English); soft-prompt tuning ~ LoRA-level cost; LID + monolingual models (WER 21.77 Hi-En); monolingual pretraining then fine-tune on small noisy CS data (MUCS 3rd place); transliteration-aware lexicons/LM from web text (MUCS CSTR/JHU). — [arXiv 2412.16507](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16507); [arXiv 2506.21576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21576v1); [Chadha et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16578); [MUCS systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05885v2)
- Multilingual DistilWhisper claims language-specific experts beat standard FT/LoRA when compressing large to small. — [arXiv 2311.01070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01070)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Ranking of levers by evidence-per-dollar for this project: (1) strong multilingual encoder init + full fine-tune of a ~0.6B FastConformer (A10G-feasible; NVIDIA's own Parakeet-v3 final fine-tune was 5k steps on 4 A100 with 7.5k h), (2) tokenizer replacement with a shared Indic+English BPE (5-8k, per SraVaani/Canary-v2) with decoder/joint re-init and encoder kept, (3) language-ID conditioning with dropout so it works when LID unknown, (4) synthetic CS via TTS+LLM text (cheap, <$50 per 22k utterances in the one published case) mixed with real data to avoid read-speech regression, (5) pseudo-labeling Telugu/Hindi unlabeled audio with an Indic-strong teacher (IndicConformer/SraVaani/Omnilingual-LLM, not Whisper-v3) with Granary-style filters, (6) distillation only after the model works.
- [INFERENCE] LoRA's appeal at A10G scale is memory/forgetting, not accuracy; for a 0.6B model, full fine-tune fits a 24 GB GPU so LoRA is optional. Use high rank (>=64) if LoRA is used, given the rank-8 penalty; keep English rehearsal data (or LoRA adapters swappable) to protect English.
- [INFERENCE] Tokenizer surgery for an already-trained transducer means retraining decoder+joint (NeMo supports excluding decoder when loading); this is cheap relative to the encoder but loses the pretrained Hindi decoder knowledge in Nemotron 3.5 if the tokenizer is replaced. Alternative: keep Nemotron's tokenizer for Hindi (supported) and only extend it for Telugu, if its tokenizer lacks Telugu coverage (not verified; tokenizer covers 8 un-fine-tuned locales).
- [INFERENCE] Teacher-output licensing: using Sarvam/Gemini/Deepgram API transcripts as pseudo-labels may breach ToS; IndicConformer (MIT) and Omnilingual (Apache 2.0) are the licence-clean teachers. Note IndicConformer has a gated download and its training-data licences are unverified.

### Gaps
- No ablation found that isolates continued fine-tuning vs LoRA vs adapters for Telugu or Hindi-English CS in a streaming transducer.
- No published Telugu-English code-switch WER from a streaming model; no open Telugu-English CS benchmark confirmed.
- No measured forgetting numbers for Indic fine-tuning of Whisper/FastConformer; no tokenizer-surgery ablation (e.g., extending vs replacing tokenizer) for transducers.
- Cost/hour of A10G compute and wall-clock for a 0.6B FastConformer fine-tune on N hours were not found in any source.

## Q3. Best published way to get streaming from an existing offline model, or to train streaming cheaply

### Takeaway
There is no peer-validated, cheap "offline FastConformer -> cache-aware streaming" recipe; NVIDIA's route is to train with limited-context masks (one model, many chunk sizes, hybrid CTC/RNNT). The cheapest credible paths are (a) start from NVIDIA's already-streaming checkpoints and fine-tune per language, (b) fine-tune an offline FastConformer with chunk-aware attention masks following the Stateful Conformer / Unified-RNNT recipes (works architecturally; recipe for fine-tuning rather than from-scratch is not documented), and (c) small Zipformer/icefall causal models. Offline-model-with-buffering hacks lose accuracy; Whisper-based conversions are English-only evidence and license-restricted.

### Cited Findings
- Buffered streaming of an offline Conformer loses accuracy vs cache-aware (RNNT test-other 11.3% at 2000 ms vs 6.3% at 1360 ms chunk-aware; offline 5.0%); cache-aware models give identical accuracy offline and streaming. — [Stateful Conformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17279v3)
- Chunk-aware (chunked limited context) masking beats regular look-ahead at the same latency; random chunk size per batch yields one multi-latency model; the paper trains models at least 200 epochs from scratch (114M params) and says nothing on fine-tuning offline checkpoints. — [Stateful Conformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17279v3)
- Released recipes: Nemotron streaming en-0.6B WER vs chunk: 8.43% (80 ms), 7.67% (160 ms), 7.07% (560 ms), 6.93% (1.12 s) average on OpenASR sets; Nemotron 3.5 Hindi FLEURS WER with LID 8.13% (80 ms) to 6.81% (1.12 s). — [Nemotron en](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b); [Nemotron 3.5](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b)
- NVIDIA Apr 2026: unified offline/streaming transducer with chunk-limited attention + right context, dynamic chunked convolutions, MCR-RNNT consistency regularization narrows the offline-streaming gap; open-sourced framework. — [arXiv 2604.19079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19079)
- A thesis on a pre-trained cache-aware FastConformer fine-tuned with cosine annealing + layer-wise LR decay reported ~10% relative WER reduction at all latency thresholds (domain adaptation of an already-streaming model, CHiME-8). — [Groningen thesis](https://campus-fryslan.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/663)
- Whisper -> streaming: WhisperRT/CarelessWhisper (arXiv 2508.12301, Aug 2025): causal encoder, LoRA on encoder and decoder, weakly-aligned data, chunks <300 ms; code/model released under CC BY-NC 4.0 (not commercial). Another paper notes UFAL LocalAgreement/SimulWhisper still rely on Whisper's non-streaming interface; a streaming-CTC-branch alternative is arXiv 2506.12154. (Note: the abstract page fetch showed the WhisperRT title, search confirms same paper.) — [arXiv 2508.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12301); [HF WhisperRT-Streaming](https://huggingface.co/MLSpeech/WhisperRT-Streaming); [arXiv 2506.12154](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12154)
- DSM/Voxtral: streaming by construction through text-stream delay; Voxtral Realtime trains a causal 970M encoder from scratch with one model serving any delay multiple of 80 ms (card lists 240 ms-2.4 s; some third-party cards say 80 ms lower bound), 4.4B total params, 15 s sliding window, vLLM-only evaluation. — [Voxtral Realtime](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11298v2)
- Qwen3-ASR streaming uses an encoder dynamic attention window and chunked re-decoding (2 s chunk, 5-token fallback, last 4 chunks unfixed); streaming costs ~0.6 WER points average on en/zh benchmarks; Hindi supported offline but streaming Hindi not reported. — [Qwen3-ASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337)
- Moonshine v2: sliding-window encoder with 80 ms lookahead; Apple M3 response latency 50-258 ms; English only, no new-language recipe. — [Moonshine v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241)
- Zipformer streaming (icefall): causal chunked encoder with chunk sizes 16/32/64/-1 and left-context 64-256 frames, trained from scratch on one L4 in the Hindi/Hinglish community model; CTC greedy 25.7% IndicVoices-hi. — [Zipformer Hindi/Hinglish card](https://huggingface.co/Vengadanathan/hindi-hinglish-zipformer-ctc-streaming)
- Local CPU deployment of NeMo streaming models: card cites quantized GGUF (q8_0) + NeMo-Speech.cpp C++ runtime; a third-party CoreML port of IndicConformer exists and a GGUF IndicConformer (Hindi, Punjabi) community port exists. No published CPU RTF numbers were found for Nemotron-streaming. — [Nemotron 3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b); [IndicConformer CoreML](https://huggingface.co/phequals/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual-coreml-rnnt); [IndicConformer GGUF](https://huggingface.co/Singla0009/IndicConformer-GGUF)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Cheapest true-streaming route: fine-tune Nemotron-3.5-streaming (already cache-aware, Hindi, LID-conditioned) rather than convert anything. If Telugu is outside its tokenizer's trained locales, expect a decoder/joint retrain with the encoder preserved.
- [INFERENCE] Converting IndicConformer-600M (hybrid CTC/RNNT FastConformer, offline) to streaming is architecturally plausible with chunked attention masks and a short fine-tune, but no published recipe or WER-loss number exists; treat as experimental. The NVIDIA unified-RNNT (MCR) paper is the best published lead and should be read in full.
- [INFERENCE] For laptop CPU, the 600M encoder at 80 ms frames with 8x subsampling is likely heavier than Moonshine-class (34-245M) or Zipformer-M (65M); quantization (int8/GGUF) is needed. Actual CPU RTF is unmeasured in sources.
- [INFERENCE] Whisper streaming conversions and DSM-family models are not viable for the CPU + commercial constraints (NC license; GPU-sized).

### Gaps
- Full text of the unified-RNNT paper (numbers, whether fine-tuned from offline checkpoints, chunk sizes) unread.
- No published offline-to-cache-aware fine-tuning recipe with ablation; no CPU latency numbers for NeMo streaming or Voxtral/Qwen3 on laptops.
- Telugu absent from every streaming model checked (Nemotron 3.5, Voxtral Realtime, Qwen3-ASR); streaming Telugu WER from any model is unknown.

## Q4. Final ranking: which lab's methodology fits best; which are not feasible

### Takeaway
Rank 1 NVIDIA NeMo/Nemotron (native streaming, 0.6B, Hindi already in, commercial licence, hundreds of hours to add a language, A10G-scale fine-tune); rank 2 AI4Bharat/IISc-ARTPARK (best open Indic data, teacher and warm-start models, plus the SraVaani SSL/alignment ideas, but offline); rank 3 next-gen Kaldi/Zipformer (cheap, streaming, CPU-friendly, but accuracy ceiling at small data); then Meta Omnilingual (teacher/pseudo-labeler, not streaming), Moonshine (architecture inspiration, English only), and Qwen3-ASR (no Telugu). Not feasible: Kyutai/Voxtral-Realtime (size, GPU, pretraining scale, no Telugu, no training code), Whisper-derived streaming (NC licence, hallucination/script issues), and closed Indian industry stacks (Sarvam, Gnani, Krutrim, Soket).

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA fit: streaming-native 600M FastConformer RNNT with language-ID conditioning and Hindi; chunk-switchable (80 ms-1.12 s); commercial licence (OpenMDW-1.1 or NVIDIA Open Model License, conflicting sources); NVIDIA's FAQ claims a few hundred hours to add a language; community 10 h Danish example. Risks: Hindi 20.2 WER on Vaani benchmark vs IndicConformer's 13.2 on the same benchmark (different sources, same named benchmark); Telugu not in the supported list. — [Nemotron 3.5](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b); [IndicConformer card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual); [NVIDIA FAQ](https://perspectives.nvidia.com/nemotron-speech/task/faq/how-much-training-data-do-i-need-to-fine-tune-a-multilingual-streaming-asr-model/)
- AI4Bharat/ARTPARK fit: MIT-licensed IndicConformer (22 languages, hybrid CTC/RNNT) and SraVaani (Telugu 25.1 mean WER, best in its table; Hindi 14.0); IndicConformer Hindi 13.8 / Telugu 26.4 mean WER (independent table); IndicWhisper 13.6 / 28.8; none streaming, none evaluated on code-switch. — [SraVaani](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235); [IndicConformer](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual); [Vistaar](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15386)
- Next-gen Kaldi: community streaming Zipformer-M Hindi on a single L4 reaches 25.7% IndicVoices-hi from 337 h (3x speed perturbation) (Apache-2.0); small models and ScaledAdam efficiency per the paper's claims. — [Zipformer card](https://huggingface.co/Vengadanathan/hindi-hinglish-zipformer-ctc-streaming); [Zipformer paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11230)
- Not feasible evidence: DSM-ASR pretraining 1.6M steps on 48 H100 over 2.5M h pseudo-labeled; Voxtral Realtime 4.4B params, 13 languages without Telugu, vLLM-only; Qwen3-ASR AuT pretraining ~40M h and Telugu absent; Omnilingual needs 4.3M h SSL pretraining (but checkpoints are released, Apache 2.0); Sarvam/Gnani are API-only with undisclosed architecture/data; Moonshine v2 English-only with 8xH100 training and no multilingual recipe; CarelessWhisper/WhisperRT CC BY-NC 4.0. — [DSM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753v2); [Voxtral Realtime](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11298v2); [Qwen3-ASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337); [Omnilingual](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09690); [Saaras v4](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/introducing-saaras-v4); [Gnani Vachana](https://huggingface.co/gnani-ai/vachana); [Moonshine v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241); [WhisperRT](https://huggingface.co/MLSpeech/WhisperRT-Streaming)
- Licence summary of artifacts checked: Voxtral Realtime Apache 2.0; Qwen3-ASR Apache 2.0; Omnilingual Apache 2.0; IndicConformer MIT; SraVaani MIT (HF card); Kyutai stt-2.6b-en weights CC-BY 4.0; Zipformer Hindi card Apache-2.0; Nemotron en NVIDIA Open Model License; Nemotron 3.5 OpenMDW-1.1 (conflict); WhisperRT CC BY-NC 4.0; Moonshine v2 unspecified "permissive". — sources as cited above.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Suggested composite plan from the evidence: (1) base = Nemotron-3.5-streaming-0.6B (or Nemotron-en + own LID conditioning); (2) teacher = IndicConformer-600M (Hindi/Telugu, MIT) and/or Omnilingual-LLM for pseudo-labeling unlabeled Hindi/Telugu/Indic-English audio with Granary-style filters (Whisper-v3 not used for Telugu); (3) tokenizer = shared Hindi+Telugu+English BPE ~5-8k units, decoder/joint re-initialised, encoder kept; (4) data = open Hindi/Telugu sets (Kathbath, IndicVoices subject to licence check, Vaani/SraVaani sources, FLEURS, MUCS, Shrutilipi/NPTEL per Vistaar) balanced with English via Canary-style temperature sampling, plus TTS+LLM synthetic code-switch data; (5) train chunk-randomised across 80-560 ms; (6) int8/GGUF export for CPU. Fallback if NeMo CPU runtime is too heavy: Zipformer-S/M streaming with distillation from the fine-tuned 0.6B model.
- [INFERENCE] Risk ranking: licence of Indic training data (unverified for IndicVoices/Shrutilipi/Vaani) is a bigger constraint than compute for "open data only, commercial".

### Gaps
- Licences of IndicVoices, Shrutilipi, Kathbath, Vaani, NPTEL, Vistaar sets for commercial use were not checked.
- Whether Nemotron 3.5's 8 un-fine-tuned locales include Telugu and whether its tokenizer covers Telugu script.
- Actual A10G throughput/time for fine-tuning a 0.6B FastConformer and CPU real-time factors for a quantized 0.6B streaming transducer on x86/Apple silicon.
- The HF blog "How to Fine-Tune Nemotron 3.5 ASR for Your Language, Domain, or Accent" (4 Jun 2026) was only seen via an excerpt/mirror; its concrete recipe should be read directly. — [mirror](https://prismix.dev/news/20bdd88a63cd)
