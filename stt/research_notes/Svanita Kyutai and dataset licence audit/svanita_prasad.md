## 0. One-paragraph verdict

Svanita is real, public, and ungated. It is a single-person (hobbyist/independent) fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3, full fp32 offline (non-streaming) model, 2.49 GB safetensors, tagged CC-BY-4.0, trained on 1,136 h of AI4Bharat IndicVoices + Kathbath (Telugu, Hindi) on one RTX 5060 Ti. The card is unusually honest: it states IndicConformer-600M is more accurate on both languages (Hindi 15.5 vs 23.7 WER; Telugu 27.4 vs 37.7), and that int8 quantisation and ONNX export broke accuracy. The licence chain as stated (Parakeet CC-BY-4.0 -> Svanita CC-BY-4.0; IndicVoices/Kathbath tagged CC-BY-4.0) looks permissive for commercial use with attribution, but we could not read the gated dataset terms (login needed) and there is no NOTICE/attribution text in the repo. Practical use for us: (b) baseline to beat: yes. (c) pseudo-label teacher: weak (worse than IndicConformer, no punctuation, Telugu 37.7% WER). (a) base model to fine-tune: legally plausible but technically pointless, because the same NVIDIA base checkpoint and the same AI4Bharat data are public, Svanita only adds a vocabulary swap + 2 stages of tuning, and it is offline with a 4,097-piece tokenizer and 8-bit-AdamW-trained weights that do not survive int8.

## 2. What Svanita is (architecture, base model, tokenizer, streaming)

From config.json / card / tokenizer.json [M]:
- Architecture: Parakeet FastConformer encoder (24 layers, hidden 1024, 8 heads, conv kernel 9, 8x subsampling, 128 mel bins, relative positions, max_position_embeddings 5000) + TDT decoder (2-layer LSTM, hidden 640; durations [0,1,2,3,4]; blank id 4096; vocab 4097). ~622M params (card: encoder 609M, decoder+joint ~13M). Pure TDT (not hybrid TDT-CTC; no CTC head).
- Base: `nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` (HF metadata `base_model:finetune:nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3`). v3 is NVIDIA's 25-European-language model (8,192-token unified tokenizer, no Indian-script coverage, no Hindi/Telugu). Encoder was initialised from v3. For v0.2 the encoder was warm-started from the author's own v0.1 checkpoint (card), which itself was v3 + Telugu training. So it is v3 -> Telugu tuning -> Telugu+Hindi tuning.
- Tokenizer: new 4,097-piece SentencePiece Unigram trained by the author on Telugu + Hindi + Latin transcripts; shipped as HF `tokenizers` Unigram (NFC normaliser, Metaspace). Our count from tokenizer.json: ~1,709 Telugu-script pieces, ~1,643 Devanagari, ~742 Latin (+ specials <unk>,<pad>,<blank>). [OURS-style inspection of tokenizer.json, 2026-10-12] Latin share is small (18%), consistent with the card's own limitation that pure English is poorly supported.
- Vocab swap recipe: only decoder embedding and joint output head re-initialised (~5.3M params); duration head, 609M encoder, decoder LSTM kept.
- Streaming? No. Svanita is an offline whole-utterance model: encoder runs once on the full clip with full attention; `transcribe_cpu.py` has no chunking, no cache, no state across chunks. The "turn" in the LinkedIn posts = a complete utterance of ~3-6 s passed in one go. Latency table in card is for 3.6-6.4 s whole clips. The card claims nothing about streaming; Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3's own model card shows only NeMo "buffered chunked" inference (chunk_secs=2 script), not cache-aware streaming, and Svanita was never trained or tested with chunking. [M for card; streaming ability U/untested]
- Audio: 16 kHz mono; ~40% of training clips degraded to 8 kHz telephone (300-3400 Hz, mu-law, 15-30 dB noise) so same model serves telephone.
- Output convention: native script for native words, English words in Latin script; lowercase except acronyms; no punctuation. Latin targets obtained from IndicVoices' annotation `native [english]` (e.g. `टाइम [time]` -> `time`); transcriber tags ([noise], [uhh], [stammers]) removed. 46% of Telugu and 39% of Hindi training clips contain Latin English. English words fused to native suffixes remain in native script.

## 3. Training data and recipe (what the card says; what it omits)

Stated [V, author; not independently verifiable, no manifests published]:
- 1,136 h, 625,184 clips: Telugu 451 h / 241,291 clips / 1,691 speakers; Hindi 685 h / 383,893 clips / 1,941 speakers (Hindi = 60% of audio).
- Sources: `ai4bharat/IndicVoices` (conversational, extempore, read) and `ai4bharat/Kathbath` (read speech; 87 h Hindi + 81 h Telugu read speech total across training per limitations section).
- Eval speakers removed from training: 125,297 Hindi and 76,692 Telugu training clips dropped because speakers overlap the validation splits. (Test = "speaker-disjoint test half" of IndicVoices `valid`, Kathbath `valid`; exact split rule/seed not published.)
- No pseudo-labelling mentioned; no synthetic data; no other corpora (no Common Voice, no Shrutilipi, no Vistaar, no Lahaja etc.). English-only audio was not included (hence weak pure English).
- Hardware: single NVIDIA RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB. Total GPU time not stated.
- Hyperparameters (v0.2): Stage A encoder frozen, 27,500 steps, lr 1e-3, 8-bit AdamW, best at 17,500. Stage B full fine-tune 61,815 steps (2 epochs), lr 3e-4 (encoder 3e-5), warmup 2,000, cosine decay, bf16 autocast, grad checkpointing, clip 1.0, best at 52,500. Stage C Telugu-weighted top-up: 12,000 steps on 70/30 Telugu/Hindi at lr 1e-4, shipped checkpoint is step 10,000 selected by Telugu dev WER. SpecAugment used (params not given). Batch size, sequence/length bucketing, seed, dropout: not stated. (v0.1: Stage A 17,680 steps, Stage B 35,359 steps = 4 epochs, shipped step 30,000, dev WER 38.6% wideband / 42.5% 8 kHz.)
- Model selection used dev WER on held-out speakers (the other half of IndicVoices valid, presumably; not stated) then the test half is reported. Potential mild selection leakage is possible but small.
- Missing from card: hours per domain/scenario beyond read-speech totals, data cleaning/filter rules, text normalisation code, batch size/effective batch, training time and cost, seeds, SpecAugment config, noise corpus for telephone augmentation, evaluation scripts, per-clip results, licence text of datasets, any attribution statement (v0.2 card dropped the "Licence and attribution" section that v0.1 had).

Chain assessment [E, not legal advice]:

Weights trained on CC-BY data are not themselves "adapted material" in an obviously restricting way, but there is legal uncertainty on whether attribution must be carried by the model. Svanita v0.1 card does credit them; v0.2 does not.
3. Uncertainties: (i) HF-gate terms of the two AI4Bharat datasets were not read (login required): need the coordinator/user to read them while logged in and capture dated text (CLAUDE.md data rule). (iv) Author gives no assurance of data provenance (manifests unpublished) and no warranty. (v) Whether the evaluation `valid` splits were truly excluded cannot be checked. (vi) Svanita's repo contains no copyright notice naming the author or NVIDIA/AI4Bharat attribution in v0.2.
4. Commercial-use verdict [E]: on stated licences, yes, commercial use of the Svanita weights looks permitted under CC-BY-4.0 provided we keep attribution to Prasad Vittaldev, NVIDIA (Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3) and AI4Bharat (IndicVoices, Kathbath). - 3 s in ~0.28 s (v0.1): card v0.1 table 3 s = 275 ms (8 thr) / 293 ms (4 thr). OK. Hardware: AMD Ryzen 5 8600G desktop, fp32, median of 10. [V]
- v0.2 ~350 ms for a 3-s turn: card gives 3.6 s -> 326 ms, 3.9 s -> 387 ms, 5.1 s -> 451 ms, 6.4 s -> 515 ms (single run, 8 thr). OK (rounded from 3.6-3.9 s). Not a streaming latency; it is time to decode a complete clip, i.e. end-of-speech-to-text latency, desktop 6-core x86 with 8 threads, NOT a laptop CPU or low-power CPU. [V]
- Hindi 23.7 vs Whisper-large-v2 fine-tune 37.3; telephone 26.5 vs 45.2; IC 15.5: all match the card exactly.

Under "either script" scoring the Whisper models are far behind anyway.
- "No Telugu numbers": WRONG for the HF card: Telugu numbers are in the card (37.7 WER v0.2, 36.5 v0.1 vs IC 27.4). The LinkedIn post may simply omit them. Flag: Svanita is ~10 WER points worse than IndicConformer RNN-T on Telugu in "either script" WER, ahead only on Latin-required WER (38.0 vs 39.1, essentially tied) and CER.
- "No test set named / no training data / hours / hardware": all are named on the HF card (IndicVoices valid speaker-disjoint half and Kathbath valid; IndicVoices+Kathbath, 1,136 h, RTX 5060 Ti). The LinkedIn posts omit them; the card does not.

## 8. Usability verdict

(a) As a commercial base model to fine-tune: legal-ish yes, practical value low. Reasons: Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 is itself CC-BY-4.0 and public, so we can reproduce the vocabulary-swap recipe on the same base ourselves with our own data and a streaming-capable architecture. What Svanita adds (Telugu+Hindi-adapted encoder + 4,097 tokenizer) is a ~1,100 h IndicVoices/Kathbath fine-tune that we can redo. It is 622M params / 2.49 GB fp32: too heavy for our laptop-CPU int8 target, and the author reports int8 and ONNX conversions failing badly (+30 WER). It is offline (full-context attention); converting a full-context Parakeet to true streaming needs retraining (cache-aware streaming FastConformer fine-tune), and v0.2's encoder is further specialised away from v3's streaming-friendly state only by fine-tuning, no architectural difference. Only potential value: a warm-started Indic encoder as initialisation to save GPU-hours (E: maybe 1-2 days of A10G), at the price of inheriting the author's training choices and unknown data cleanliness. Provenance risk is moderate: single-person project, no code/manifests, 19 days old, no versioning promise, no warranty.
(b) As a baseline to beat: yes, useful and cheap. It is a real, public, reproducible reference for offline Telugu/Hindi code-mixed CPU-friendly TDT (fp32). But the stronger baseline for accuracy is IndicConformer-600M (MIT, 15.5 / 27.4 WER by the author's own table) and the reference for our own metric should be re-measured on OUR test sets and OUR normaliser [OURS pending]. Do not quote the author's numbers as ours. Beating Svanita is a low bar on accuracy; its distinctive feature to match is Latin-script English in code-mixed output and the 40% telephone augmentation.
(c) As a pseudo-label teacher: weak. Worse WER than IndicConformer in both languages (esp. read speech: Telugu Kathbath 39.7 vs 20.9; Hindi 25.4 vs 8.9), no punctuation/casing, thin pure-English ability, occasional wrong-script outputs without `--lang`. Teacher outputs are CC-BY-derived, so allowed by licence, but it would propagate IndicVoices/Kathbath biases and gives no signal beyond the data it was trained on. Only worth considering as a Latin-script-English labelling helper for code-mixed audio (its Latin retention is the one thing it does well), ideally ensembled/filtered against IndicConformer; verify on our dev set first.
