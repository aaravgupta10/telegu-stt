## Kyutai Pocket TTS model page (`huggingface.co/kyutai/pocket-tts`)
- Licence tag **cc-by-4.0**; "6 languages" (English, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Spanish; no Hindi or Telugu); ~100M parameters; ~200 ms to first audio; ~6x real time on a MacBook Air M4 using 2 CPU cores; voice cloning from a wav prompt; Python 3.10-3.14, PyTorch 2.5+, no GPU needed for inference.
- **Gate:** default "share your contact information" prompt **plus a prohibited-use text** shown before the button **"Agree and access repository"** (the button is present for the signed-in account; **not clicked**). The prohibited-use text says use must not result in or facilitate illegal, harmful, deceptive, fraudulent or unauthorized activity, and lists as prohibited, among others: voice impersonation or cloning without explicit and lawful consent; misinformation or disinformation (fake news, fraudulent calls, presenting generated content as genuine recordings); and unlawful, harmful, libelous, abusive, harassing, discriminatory, hateful or privacy-invasive content. They "disclaim all liability for any non-compliant use".
- The card says non-English languages also have **bigger 24-layer variants** ("higher quality but slower", for example `--language italian_24l`), that **training code was released in August 2026**, and that a separate page details the licence of each voice (not opened).

## Pocket TTS training README (`github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts`, `training/README.md`, read via raw.githubusercontent.com)
- **Requirements:** Linux (no official Windows/Mac training support); one NVIDIA GPU; the default batch wants ~56 GB, while a consumer GPU runs `batch_size: 16` with `grad_accum_steps: 4` in ~16 GB; more GPUs via `torchrun`; ~60 GB disk per 1,000 h of prepared audio; Python 3.10+ and `uv`.
- **Data:** speech paired with transcripts **plus word-level alignments** (start and end of each word). "You can get something working with 100 hours of data, but for a strong model, you'll want 1000+ hours." "Use 200 hours of audio for a model that speaks, 2000 for one that actually sounds good." Prefer hand-transcribed over auto-transcribed data.
- **Alignment:** forced alignment with a CTC variant using **Wav2Vec2, which is language-specific, so a version fine-tuned for the target language is needed** (their Czech example used `comodoro/wav2vec2-xls-r-300m-cs-250`); alternative: an ASR with timestamps such as `whisper-timestamped` (noisier transcripts). They also train a text tokenizer (`train_tokenizer.py`).
- **Recipes:** `scratch.yaml` (24-layer teacher from scratch), `depth_distill.yaml` (distil to a 6-layer student; also bakes in classifier-free guidance), `finetune.yaml` (continue the English teacher on more English data), **`finetune_language.yaml`** (continue from the English teacher but **re-initialise the text embedding** because the tokenizer differs; **on Czech it reached the same WER about 2.5x sooner than from scratch**). Training in two steps (teacher then distil) "works better than training a 6-layer model from scratch".
- **Official timings (2,000 h of HiFiTTS-2, effective batch 64):** teacher, 1x H100-80GB: 6.3 steps/s, **54 GiB peak**, 200k steps in ~8.8 h; 2x H100 (batch 32 each): 8.1 steps/s, 30 GiB each; 4x H100: 9.8 steps/s, 18 GiB each; 8x H100: 10.4 steps/s, 12 GiB each. Precompute of latents (once): ~50 min on 1 GPU for 2,000 h; ~6 GB of latents per 1,000 h. **Distillation:** 1x H100: 10.5 steps/s, **9.8 GiB**, 200k steps in ~5.3 h; 8x H100: 21.3 steps/s.
- **Hyperparameter notes (for from-scratch runs):** learning rate 2e-4, effective batch >= 64, `flow_batch_multiplier` 4, lognormal t-sampling; the quality "transition" takes a long time to appear and is not always reached; `model.safetensors` saved is an EMA of the weights.
- **Evaluation they use:** WER (an ASR reads the generated speech back; needs an ASR for the language), speaker similarity (`microsoft/wavlm-base-plus-sv`), and **UTMOS** (audio-quality predictor; "might be less accurate for other languages"). Their English results: WER ~0.8-0.9%, UTMOS ~4.3.
- Not read (page truncated): the final "Distribution" part beyond the first lines.

## Prasad Vittaldev's Telugu model card (`prasadvittaldev/pocket-tts-telugu-female-syspin`)
- Licence tag **MIT** ("matching upstream pocket-tts"; the training corpora "carry their own licences, check the dataset pages above before commercial use"). ~110M parameters, 24 kHz, **one fixed voice (`syspin_female`)**; voice cloning did not survive distillation (output stayed within a 9 Hz band across prompts of 137-231 Hz).
- **Speed (his, desktop x86 CPU):** **5.5x real time with int8** (`--quantize`) versus 3.5x for float. ONNX bundle via `KevinAHM/pocket-tts-onnx-export`: int4 3.98x, int8 4.08x, float32 3.06x on 4 threads; sizes 86 MB (int4 bundle), 139 MB (int8), 419 MB (fp32). Runtimes he names: ONNX Runtime Web (browser) and **sherpa-onnx for Android/iOS/Raspberry Pi** [his claim, unverified]. Dynamic int8 is "indistinguishable"; int4 "usable"; int3 and below lose words.
- **How it was made [his account]:** teacher = Kyutai's released **24-layer English model** fine-tuned on **124 h of Telugu, 15,000 steps**, text embedding reset against a Telugu SentencePiece tokenizer; **word-level forced alignment in native Telugu script** (method not stated; he says Meta's MMS aligner is unusable because it romanises); distillation to 6 layers for **40,000 steps on one speaker's 53.0 h / 20,599 clips**.
- **Data credited:** `SPRINGLab/IndicTTS_Telugu` and `arpit-tiwari/syspin-telugu-tts` (SYSPIN, IISc). **From the dataset pages: IndicTTS_Telugu has 8.58k rows (female); the SYSPIN re-upload has 39.2k train + 4.35k test rows (~43.5k clips, two voices).** Rough arithmetic [E]: 20,599 clips = 53 h implies ~9.3 s per clip, so ~43.5k SYSPIN clips is ~112 h; with IndicTTS (~8.7 h) that is about the 124 h, so the **124 h is most likely IndicTTS Telugu plus SYSPIN Telugu (both voices)**.
- **Important practical note on his card:** generate **one sentence at a time** and join the audio. Pocket TTS locates sentence ends using tokens from its own tokenizer, an Indic tokenizer does not produce those, so for Telugu long text is pushed through as one chunk and **silently drops words**. Split on `.`, `?`, `!` before calling the model.
- The English-side voice prompt in the example is from `kyutai/tts-voices`.

## SYSPIN (IISc), primary source (`vaani.iisc.ac.in/dataset/syspindataset`, `syspin.iisc.ac.in/terms-and-conditions`)
- "The corpus, created by IISc Bengaluru, is released under a **CC-BY-4.0** license." Studio-recorded TTS data; **920 hours, 9 languages, 18 speakers, 462,311 sentences, 48 kHz 24-bit**; cite "Abhayjeet et al., SYSPIN_S1.0 Corpus - A TTS Corpus of 900+ hours in nine Indian languages, 2025". Download button leads to `spiredatasets.ee.iisc.ac.in/syspincorpus` (a form; **not opened, not submitted**).
- The website terms and conditions govern use of the website (they collect first name, last name, organisation, email, optional phone, forbid scraping of the website); they do not restate the dataset licence.
- A web search also surfaced that the older LIMMITS challenge releases of Hindi and Telugu TTS data were reported by the organisers to contain errors (audio distortion, wrong pronunciations, text-audio mismatch), so **check quality** of any release used.
- **The HF re-upload `arpit-tiwari/syspin-telugu-tts` shows no licence tag on its page; use the official IISc download instead.**

## Rasa (`huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/Rasa`)
- Licence tag **cc-by-4.0**, and the card's own "License" section says **CC-BY-4.0** (consistent). Default contact-sharing gate (not accepted). Funded by Bhashini (MeitY), supported by EkStep and Nilekani Philanthropies. 44 speaker-language pairs, 48 kHz mono, minimum ~20 h per speaker; neutral readings (Wikipedia, IndicTTS texts), the six Ekman emotions, command-style speech (Alexa, BigBasket, UMANG, DigiPay), conversations, news and narration.
- **Hours:** **Telugu female 27.28 h (15,404 utterances), Telugu male 24.98 h (15,006)** = 52.26 h; **Hindi female 27.05 h (15,107), Hindi male 23.78 h (13,464)** = 50.83 h.
